A sketch draws in three dimensions with the verbs of the solids, in the same window as everything else. There is nothing to switch on: the first `box` or `sphere` gives the window a camera, a light and a floor of depth, and a 2D verb after it draws flat on top.

```roci picture
background (rgb 16 20 38)
plane at 0 100 0 size 600 600 fill (rgb 44 54 92)
sphere at -110 40 0 radius 60 fill orange
box at 90 40 0 size 90 fill teal
fill white
text "a sphere and a box" at 20 20
```

Drag across the window and the camera turns around the middle; the wheel brings it closer. That is the default camera, and it steps aside when the sketch reads the mouse or the pointer is over a widget.

## Where things are

The world is the window's. Its middle is the middle of the window, `x` goes right, `y` goes **down**, as it does on the flat canvas, and `z` comes out of the screen towards you. On the plane `z = 0`, seen straight on, one unit is one pixel, so a sphere of radius 50 is as wide as a circle of radius 50. `axes` draws the three directions, red, green and blue, and `grid` a floor of lines to judge distances by:

```roci picture
background (rgb 16 20 38)
fill (rgb 70 84 130)
grid size 500 lines 10
axes size 150
```

Every solid takes a place after `at`, three numbers or a vector of three (`{ 0, 40, 0 }`), and its middle goes there.

## The solids

`box` takes a side or three sizes, `sphere` and `icosphere` a radius, `cone`, `cylinder` and `capsule` a radius and a height (they stand upright), `torus` the radius of its ring and of its `tube`, and `plane` a flat square with its width and depth, lying like a floor:

```roci picture
background (rgb 16 20 38)
turn 14 around x
box at -210 -70 0 size 100 fill tomato
sphere at -70 -70 0 radius 55 fill gold
cone at 70 -70 0 radius 50 height 110 fill teal
cylinder at 210 -70 0 radius 48 height 110 fill orchid
capsule at -210 90 0 radius 40 height 130 fill skyblue
torus at -70 90 0 radius 52 tube 18 fill orange
icosphere at 70 90 0 radius 55 fill lightgreen
push [
  move 210 90 0
  turn 65 around x
  plane at 0 0 0 size 120 120 fill white
]
```

A solid is painted by the fill, like a flat shape, and a picture in the fill paints it all over (`box at 0 0 0 size 100 fill photo`): a box shows the picture on each face, a sphere wraps it from pole to pole.

## Moving, turning and growing

`move`, `turn` and `grow` take a third number in space. `move x y z` moves everything drawn after it, `turn degrees around x` (or `y` or `z`) turns it about that axis, and `grow x y z` stretches it. `push [ ]` puts them back afterwards, so a block can place a group of solids and leave the rest alone:

```roci
draw [
  background (rgb 16 20 38)
  sphere at 0 30 0 radius 50 fill gold
  for i from 1 to 6 [
    push [
      turn (i * 60 + frame) around y
      move 150 40 0
      turn (frame * 2) around x
      box at 0 0 0 size 50 fill (hsb (i * 40) 170 235)
    ]
  ]
]
```

A `move` with two numbers and a plain `turn` are still the flat ones, for what is drawn on top.

## The camera

The orbit is the camera until a sketch says otherwise. `camera at x y z` puts the eye somewhere and `look at x y z` aims it, and either takes the camera off the mouse for that frame, so a sketch that moves its own camera writes them in `draw`:

```roci
draw [
  background (rgb 16 20 38)
  turned = frame * 0.5
  camera at (cos turned * 560) -260 (sin turned * 560)
  look at 0 -30 0
  grid size 640 lines 16
  box at 0 -30 0 size 60 fill teal
]
```

`ortho` draws without perspective, everything the same size at every distance, and `perspective` puts it back (`perspective fov 70` sees wider). Two verbs go between the scene and the window: `screen point { x, y, z }` gives the pixel where a place of the scene shows, so a label can follow a solid, and `world point mouse` gives the place on the floor (the plane `y = 0`) under a pixel.

## Lights and materials

Without a `light` the scene has a sun and a little gray light from everywhere. The first `light` of a frame takes those away, so a sketch that writes its own writes all of them: `light ambient` from everywhere, `light sun from x y z` from far away in a direction, `light point at x y z` from a place, weaker further away (`falloff` says how far it reaches), and `light spot at ... to ...` a cone of light with an `angle`. Each takes a color with `fill`.

`material` says what the solids drawn after it are made of: `metallic` from 0 to 1, `roughness` from 0 (a mirror's sharp highlight) to 1 (chalk), and `emissive` for light of its own. `no lights` draws what follows flat in its color, as for the bulb of a lamp:

```roci picture
background (rgb 10 12 24)
light ambient fill (rgb 52 58 92)
light sun from -0.5 -0.8 0.4 fill (rgb 110 120 160)
light point at -170 -90 120 fill (rgb 255 150 70) falloff 220
fill (rgb 120 128 150)
material roughness 0.9
plane at 0 100 0 size 700 520
material metallic 0 roughness 0.55
sphere at -170 40 0 radius 60 fill (rgb 235 235 235)
material metallic 1 roughness 0.25
sphere at -20 40 0 radius 60 fill (rgb 235 190 90)
material metallic 1 roughness 0.6
sphere at 130 40 0 radius 60 fill (rgb 190 200 215)
no lights
sphere at -170 -90 120 radius 8 fill (rgb 255 190 120)
```

## Shadows

`shadows` turns them on for the frame. The first sun or spot light casts them, and the first point light too, in every direction, and every solid casts and receives them:

```roci picture
background (rgb 14 16 30)
light ambient fill (rgb 46 52 86)
light sun from -0.7 -0.55 0.45 fill (rgb 250 240 225)
light point at 0 -60 150 fill (rgb 255 140 70) falloff 160
shadows
fill (rgb 128 136 156)
material roughness 0.95
plane at 0 100 0 size 760 700
material roughness 0.5
for i from 0 to 5 [
  push [
    turn (i * 60) around y
    move 190 55 0
    box at 0 0 0 size 80 fill (hsb (i * 40) 150 235)
  ]
]
sphere at 0 40 0 radius 60 fill (rgb 240 200 90)
```

## Models

`model` draws a model from the sketch's `assets/` folder by name: a glTF file (`.glb`, or `.gltf` with its files) or an OBJ file with the `.mtl` it names, in the colors, metal, roughness and glow of its own materials and the pictures they are painted with. Its middle goes at the place, and `size` is its longest side, whatever units it was made in:

```roci
draw [
  background (rgb 14 16 30)
  shadows
  plane at 0 120 0 size 800 700 fill (rgb 128 136 156)
  turn (frame * 0.7) around y
  model "striker.obj" at 0 0 0 size 380
]
```

A file saved again while the sketch runs is read again. Blender's own `.blend` files and FBX are not read: export glTF or OBJ from them.

## Meshes and wireframes

`mesh` draws triangles a sketch makes: a list of places, three for each triangle, lit as a solid is. `as wireframe` draws only its edges, as thin tubes `thickness` across, and leaves out the edge between two triangles that lie flat in one plane, so a square made of two triangles shows four edges, not five:

```roci picture
background (rgb 16 20 38)
light ambient fill (rgb 70 78 110)
light sun from -0.6 -0.7 0.5
top = { 0, -110, 0 }
a = { -90, 40, -50 }
b = { 90, 40, -50 }
c = { 0, 40, 90 }
gem = [ a b top b c top c a top a c b ]
push [
  move -150 0 0
  turn 20 around y
  mesh gem fill orange
]
push [
  move 150 0 0
  turn 20 around y
  thickness 3
  mesh gem as wireframe fill white
]
```

## The turtle in space

`turtle space` makes the turtle walk the scene for the rest of the frame. `forward`, `back`, `left` and `right` work as on the flat canvas, and three more turn it in space: `up` and `down` tip its nose, and `roll` turns it about the way it faces. Its trail is a tube in the stroke color, lit and casting shadows, so a recursive verb grows a tree (see [the turtle](../11-the-turtle/)):

```roci picture
learn branch length depth [
  if depth is 0 [ stop ]
  thickness (1.2 + depth * 1.6)
  stroke (hsb (85 - depth * 10) 190 (240 - depth * 22))
  forward length
  for i from 0 to 2 [
    push [
      roll (i * 120 + depth * 25)
      up 32
      branch (length * 0.74) (depth - 1)
    ]
  ]
]

background (rgb 14 16 30)
shadows
plane at 0 175 0 size 800 700 fill (rgb 120 128 150)
move 0 175 0
turtle space
branch 85 6
```

## Collision in space

The collision verbs of [collision](../09-collision/) take shapes with three numbers: a vector of three is a point, `pos` and `radius` a sphere, `pos` and `size` a box (with an `angle` of three turns), `from` and `to` a segment and `triangles` a mesh. `ray mouse` gives the line from the camera through the pointer, so `cast (ray mouse) things` finds what is under it, and `triangles "ship.glb" at 0 0 0 size 300` gives the triangles of a model as `model` would draw it.

## Flat on top

Whatever order they are written in, the solids are drawn first and the flat verbs over them, in the window's pixels: text, widgets and shapes stay where they are while the camera moves. The [examples](../../examples/) have a section of 3D sketches to read and run.
