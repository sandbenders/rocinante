a language for prototyping, testing and shipping great ideas, with a fast and modern engine under it

rocinante draws on more than 20 years of research, teaching and practice in creative coding and creative AI, and is made for a symbiotic future, where people and machines make things together and people stay in charge of what's made. Its design has been tested in classrooms and in professional performances and exhibitions around the world. It makes apps, games, pictures, animations, sounds and interactive pieces, in a language small enough to learn in an afternoon.

change it while it runs

Save the file and the window changes while it runs. Mistakes never stop it: the window keeps the last good picture and says, in plain words, what went wrong and what would work.

shapes, color and gradients

Circles, rectangles, arcs, polygons with holes and whole SVG drawings, with outlines that have caps and joins. Colors by name, hex, RGB, HSB or OKLCH, and gradients mixed in Oklab, so they never go muddy.

Shapes and color The landscape example

pictures, canvases and shaders

Pictures and their pixels, canvases that keep what you draw, blend modes, clips and masks, built-in effects such as glow, and shaders of your own that reload as you save. Any frame can be saved as PNG, SVG or PDF, or recorded as a movie.

Pictures Canvases Shaders The tunnel example

3D in the same window

Boxes, spheres, cones, tori and meshes beside the 2D, a camera that orbits by itself, lights, materials and shadows, glTF and OBJ models with their pictures, and the turtle in three dimensions.

3D The lamp example

sound you can draw with

Sound files that play and loop, long songs read as they go, instruments of your own and SoundFonts, sixteen effects and the microphone. How loud and how high a sound is are values a drawing reads. MIDI in and out, OSC for other programs, and what plays recorded to a file.

Sound The music example

widgets anyone can use

Buttons, sliders, knobs, pads, text boxes, lists and color pickers as plain verbs, in any sketch. They work with a keyboard, a mouse or a finger, remember their values from one run to the next, and are read aloud by screen readers.

Widgets The dials example

games

Scenes for menus and levels, collision between any shapes, gamepads and joysticks that players can remap, and scores kept from one run to the next, sealed in a vault when nobody should edit them. WILDVEX, at the top of this page, is made this way.

Scenes Collision Remembering Gamepads

the world outside

Data files that read as records you can use at once (JSON, CSV, TSV, TOML and text), the system's file dialogs, the web, UDP, TCP and web sockets, the webcam and movies. Nothing waits: the window keeps drawing while an answer comes.

Data Dialogs The web Webcam Movies

apps for every system

One command compiles a sketch to native code, drawing exactly the frames it draws while you work, and makes it an app: a Mac app, Linux packages (.deb, RPM, Arch and AppImage), FreeBSD packages, OpenBSD packages, a Windows installer (with a zip and an MSIX package) and an Android app (a signed APK), each with its icons drawn for every system from one picture. A compiled sketch also goes straight onto an iPhone or an Android phone that is plugged in.

what is under it

graphics
Vulkan, Metal and Direct3D 12, through SDL3 and its GPU API, with full Wayland support on Linux, FreeBSD and OpenBSD and X11 too. Shapes are batched on the GPU: 100,000 circles a frame at 60 frames a second.
everywhere
macOS, Windows, Linux, FreeBSD and OpenBSD, with the same sketch behaving the same on each.
live, then compiled
While you work, sketches are interpreted and reload the moment you save, keeping their values. Compiled, they become native code that draws the same pictures, pixel for pixel.
open
Free software under the Zero-Clause BSD license, the most permissive there is, written in Odin.

and everything else

text
Fonts and text in any script, laid out and wrapped.
movement
Tweens with seven easing curves, timers, and transforms that move, turn, grow and shear.
mouse, keys and fingers
Every button by name, the wheel, keys pressed and held, and every finger with how hard it presses.
lists, records and tables
Keep, skip, collect, combine, find, sort and shuffle; thousands of particles in a few lines.
your own verbs
Teach it new words with learn; every kind of value is worked out for you.
the turtle
Logo's turtle in every window, for branching trees and fractals.
many windows
As many as you like, each with its own drawing, sending values to one another.
noise and chance
Perlin and simplex noise and seeded random numbers, the same on every system.
folders and libraries
A sketch folder with git set up, and other people's libraries from any git address.
checks and snapshots
Checks you write beside your sketch, and its picture compared with the one you kept.
errors that teach
Every mistake found in one go and said in plain words, with a line that would work.
tools
roci fmt, check, man and test, and the same verbs from Odin and C.

start

  1. roci new my-sketch

    makes a sketch folder with one circle in it

  2. cd my-sketch
  3. roci run

    opens the window; change sketch.roci and save, and rocinante reloads it while it runs

rocinante

Four centuries later the name went to a ship. In The Expanse, the novel series by James S. A. Corey (the pen name of Daniel Abraham and Ty Franck) that began with Leviathan Wakes in 2011, the Rocinante starts out as the Tachi, a Martian navy gunship. The survivors of the ice freighter Canterbury take it over and rename it Rocinante, and it carries them through all nine novels and the television series (Syfy from 2015, then Amazon Prime Video). It is a small ship that keeps flying when it has no business doing so.
